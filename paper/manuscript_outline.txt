Working title
Structure-based chemical product-category scoring from molecular structure: full-positive PubChem broad-category rebuild, QED comparison, and desktop deployment

Target paper shape
1. Introduction
   - Why chemical product-category suggestion from structure matters.
   - Why a score-based method is a reasonable first publication target before adding the machine-learning-final models.
   - Why PubChem Classification Browser enables broad category expansion.
   - Why QED is the natural baseline: all scorers were inspired by RDKit drug-likeness style scoring, so improvement beyond QED is the minimum publication gate.

2. Data and benchmark setup
   - Positive set source: PubChem Classification Browser HID 72 chemical classes.
   - Final reported categories: animal drugs, human drugs, cosmetics, flavoring agents, food additives, food contact substances, fragrances, pesticides, solvents, surfactants.
   - Final run uses all available positives for those categories in this project run.
   - Positive count reported in the final tables means unique SMILES after PubChem CID-to-SMILES fetch and SMILES deduplication.
   - Negative construction for the final run:
     - negatives are drawn from the other final categories,
     - cross-category overlap is retained,
     - removal only for exact target-positive overlap, duplicate negative SMILES, and near-positive Tanimoto matches.

3. Scoring methodology
   - Kim Nayeon: pesticide baseline with PPV histogram plus scaffold/residue logic.
   - Lee Seoyun: Bayesian optimization around property-score separation.
   - Choi Yebin: automated category builder using Tanimoto negative filtering, KS-based property selection, structural-pattern screening, and weight optimization.
   - Final broad-category rebuild in this project combines:
     - positive-set physicochemical property ranges,
     - structural patterns,
     - retained cross-category negatives,
     - and Lee-style Bayesian optimization when it beats grid search.

4. Main reporting rule
   - A category is manuscript-reportable only if its scorer is compared against raw RDKit QED on the same rebuilt evaluation set.
   - Main performance table should always show:
     - scorer AUC,
     - scorer balanced accuracy,
     - QED AUC,
     - QED balanced accuracy,
     - delta vs QED.

5. Final results to report
   - Surfactants: AUC 0.9778 vs QED 0.2716.
   - Flavoring agents: AUC 0.9389 vs QED 0.5656.
   - Solvents: AUC 0.9295 vs QED 0.5197.
   - Fragrances: AUC 0.9254 vs QED 0.5527.
   - Food additives: AUC 0.8334 vs QED 0.4463.
   - Human drugs: AUC 0.7876 vs QED 0.4730.
   - Pesticides: AUC 0.7527 vs QED 0.6790.
   - Food contact substances: AUC 0.6213 vs QED 0.3497.
   - Cosmetics: AUC 0.6179 vs QED 0.2605.
   - Animal drugs: AUC 0.6136 vs QED 0.4445.

6. Interpretation section
   - Full-positive rebuild changed some categories materially relative to earlier capped runs.
   - Pesticides improved once the positive cap was removed and now beats QED under the final rebuilt regime.
   - Several broad product categories remain weaker than fragrance/surfactant/solvent classes, but they still beat QED and therefore remain reportable.
   - Food contact substances and cosmetics remain difficult broad classes even after full-positive rebuild, which should be stated directly.

7. Desktop deployment section
   - Desktop app now has a final broad-category rebuild line for the 10 selected categories.
   - Report the exact model registry used for the manuscript run.
   - Keep machine-learning-final models out of this paper version.

8. Limitations
   - Positive counts are full available project-run positives after CID-to-SMILES deduplication, not raw PubChem CID counts.
   - The final task is still positive-versus-constructed-background, not perfect binary truth.
   - Negative-set definition still matters and should be described explicitly in Methods.
   - QED comparison is same-regime comparison, not universal proof of deployment readiness.

9. Conclusion
   - The final uncapped broad-category rebuild is ready for manuscript drafting.
   - All 10 final categories are now paired with explicit QED comparisons.
   - The manuscript can claim score improvement beyond QED on the rebuilt evaluation sets.
