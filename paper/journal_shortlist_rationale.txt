Journal-shortlist rationale with quality and precedent

Decision rule
I ranked journals by three things together:
1. scope fit
2. quality bar / typical methodological standard in the journal
3. precedent: whether similar scorer / structural-alert / cheminformatics methodology papers have appeared there

Important caution
- No one can guarantee publication before peer review.
- The strongest defensible claim is: this manuscript has a realistic fit for some journals and a weak fit for others.

Current manuscript strengths
- full-positive PubChem HID72 rebuild for 10 broad categories
- retained-cross-category negative policy stated explicitly
- property-range + structural-pattern hybrid score
- same-regime QED comparison for every final category
- deployable desktop implementation

Current manuscript weakness
- novelty is practical/methodological, not a brand-new theoretical framework
- no external experimental validation
- some categories are only moderate performers

Impact-factor / quality context
- Journal of Cheminformatics: JIF 7.9 (2025), 5-year JIF 10.0
  Source: https://link.springer.com/journal/13321
- Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling (JCIM): 2-year Impact Factor 5.3 (2025), CiteScore 9.8
  Source: https://pubs.acs.org/journal/jcisd8 and https://pubs.acs.org/page/jcisd8/about.html
- Molecular Informatics: Journal Impact Factor 3.1, CiteScore 5.9
  Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/18681751
- BMC Chemistry: JIF 5.1 (2025), 5-year JIF 5.5
  Source: https://link.springer.com/journal/13065

QED precedent
- QED itself was published in Nature Chemistry:
  Bickerton et al., “Quantifying the chemical beauty of drugs”, Nature Chemistry 4, 90–98 (2012).
  Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/nchem.1243
- This matters because your manuscript is explicitly using QED as the main baseline and is closest in spirit to “molecular desirability / structure-based scoring” work.
- But Nature Chemistry is not the realistic target comparator for acceptance probability; it is precedent for concept, not target-journal choice.

Why Journal of Cheminformatics is the strongest first target
Scope evidence
- Official scope explicitly includes chemical structure representations, structure/substructure/similarity searching, QSAR, databases, and data mining / molecular modelling.
- That is almost exactly what this manuscript does.

Quality / precedent evidence
Similar studies already published there include:
1. Ertl & Schuffenhauer, “Estimation of synthetic accessibility score of drug-like molecules based on molecular complexity and fragment contributions” (J Cheminform, 2009)
   https://doi.org/10.1186/1758-2946-1-8
2. Sorokina & Steinbeck, “NaPLeS: a natural products likeness scorer—web application and database” (J Cheminform, 2019)
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-019-0378-z
3. Cortes-Ciriano, “Bioalerts: a python library for the derivation of structural alerts from bioactivity and toxicity data sets” (J Cheminform, 2016)
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-016-0125-7

Why this is strong evidence
- Those papers show the journal already accepts:
  - molecule-level scoring methods
  - structural-alert methods
  - fragment/pattern-based interpretable scoring
  - software-backed cheminformatics tools
- Your manuscript sits directly in that lane.

Decision
- Best combination of scope fit, precedent, and realistic quality bar.

Why JCIM is plausible but riskier
Scope evidence
- JCIM covers chemical informatics, molecular modeling, AI/ML on chemical data, computational methods, pattern recognition, and similarity methods.

Precedent evidence
Similar studies in JCIM include:
1. Ertl, Roggo, Schuffenhauer, “Natural Product-likeness Score and Its Application for Prioritization of Compound Libraries” (JCIM, 2008)
   https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ci700286x
2. Huang et al., “RASA: A Rapid Retrosynthesis-Based Scoring Method for the Assessment of Synthetic Accessibility of Drug-like Molecules” (JCIM, 2011)
   https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ci100216g
3. Ellison et al., “Assessment of Methods To Define the Applicability Domain of Structural Alert Models” (JCIM, 2011)
   https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ci1000967

Why it is harder
- JCIM often expects more obvious methodological novelty than a carefully engineered applied pipeline.
- Your work is credible here, but reviewers may ask what is fundamentally new beyond a good benchmark/rebuild/deployment combination.

Decision
- Good second target, but not as safe as Journal of Cheminformatics.

Why Molecular Informatics is a good backup
Scope evidence
- It covers molecular informatics, QSAR, modelling, and computer-assisted molecular design.

Precedent evidence
Relevant Molecular Informatics studies include:
1. Orogo et al., “Construction and Consensus Performance of (Q)SAR Models for Predicting Phospholipidosis Using a Dataset of 743 Compounds” (Molecular Informatics, 2012)
   https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/minf.201200048
2. Polishchuk, “Universal Approach for Structural Interpretation of QSAR/QSPR Models” (Molecular Informatics, 2013)
   https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/minf.201300029
3. Buchwald et al., “Using Local Models to Improve (Q)SAR Predictivity” (Molecular Informatics, 2011)
   https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/minf.201000154

Why it works
- This venue clearly publishes interpretable QSAR / structural-pattern / local-model work.
- Your paper fits that family well.
- The quality bar looks more compatible than JCIM if reviewers judge the paper as solid but not radically novel.

Decision
- Strong backup, probably safer than JCIM if novelty objections arise.

Why BMC Chemistry is only a fallback
Scope evidence
- Broad chemistry scope; accepts sound computational chemistry-related work.

Quality / precedent evidence
- It has chemistry/QSAR validation papers, but it is not the natural home for specialist cheminformatics scoring methods.
- Example relevant paper: “Comparison of various methods for validity evaluation of QSAR models” (BMC Chemistry, 2022)
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13065-022-00856-4

Decision
- Acceptable fallback if specialist journals decline, but weaker audience match.

Final recommendation
1. Journal of Cheminformatics
   - strongest scope fit
   - strongest direct precedent for scorer / alert / cheminformatics software papers
   - highest impact factor among the realistic specialist fits in this shortlist
2. JCIM
   - higher prestige than Molecular Informatics, but higher novelty risk
3. Molecular Informatics
   - very reasonable specialist backup with solid precedent
4. BMC Chemistry
   - broad fallback
